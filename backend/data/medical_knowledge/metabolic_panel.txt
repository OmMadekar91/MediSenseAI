# Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP)

A Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP) is a blood test measuring 14 different substances to evaluate body metabolism, kidney function, liver health, fluid and electrolyte balance, and blood sugar.

## 1. Kidney & Waste Markers
- **Serum Creatinine**: Waste product produced by muscle breakdown and filtered by healthy kidneys.
  - Typical adult range: 0.7 to 1.3 mg/dL for men; 0.6 to 1.1 mg/dL for women.
  - Elevated Creatinine: Indicates reduced kidney filtration, acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease (CKD), dehydration, or high muscle breakdown.
- **Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)**: Waste product from protein digestion processed by the liver and removed by the kidneys.
  - Typical adult range: 7 to 20 mg/dL.
  - Elevated BUN: Dehydration, high protein diet, kidney dysfunction, or gastrointestinal bleeding.
- **Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)**: Calculated metric estimating kidney filtering capacity.
  - Normal: 90 mL/min/1.73m2 or higher.
  - 60 to 89: Mild reduction (normal in older adults if without kidney damage).
  - Below 60: May indicate chronic kidney disease if sustained for 3 or more months.

## 2. Electrolytes and Fluid Balance
- **Sodium (Na)**: 135 to 145 mEq/L (or mmol/L). Regulates fluid balance and nerve transmission.
  - Hyponatremia (<135): Water retention, diuretic medications, heart failure.
  - Hypernatremia (>145): Dehydration, excessive sodium intake.
- **Potassium (K)**: 3.5 to 5.0 mEq/L (or mmol/L). Vital for cardiac rhythm and muscle contraction.
  - Hypokalemia (<3.5): Diuretics, vomiting, diarrhea; risk of cardiac arrhythmias.
  - Hyperkalemia (>5.0): Kidney impairment, certain medications; potentially dangerous cardiac arrhythmias.
- **Chloride (Cl)**: 96 to 106 mEq/L. Maintains osmotic pressure and acid-base balance.
- **Carbon Dioxide / Bicarbonate (CO2 / HCO3)**: 23 to 29 mEq/L. Indicates body acid-base balance.
- **Calcium (Ca)**: 8.5 to 10.2 mg/dL. Important for bone strength, muscle contraction, and cardiac function.

## 3. Proteins
- **Total Protein**: 6.0 to 8.3 g/dL. Includes albumin and globulin fractions.
- **Albumin**: 3.5 to 5.0 g/dL. Primary protein produced by the liver maintaining oncotic pressure. Low levels occur in malnutrition, liver cirrhosis, or nephrotic syndrome.
