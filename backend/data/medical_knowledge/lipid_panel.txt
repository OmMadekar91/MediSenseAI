# Lipid Panel & Cholesterol Guidelines

A lipid panel measures fats (lipids) in the blood to evaluate cardiovascular risk, including coronary artery disease, heart attack, and stroke.

## 1. Key Lipid Markers & Target Ranges (Adults)

- **Total Cholesterol**: Combined measurement of LDL, HDL, and other lipid components.
  - Desirable: Less than 200 mg/dL (or <5.2 mmol/L).
  - Borderline high: 200 to 239 mg/dL.
  - High: 240 mg/dL or higher.

- **Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL) Cholesterol ("Bad" Cholesterol)**: Carries cholesterol to tissues; excess contributes to atherosclerotic plaque buildup.
  - Optimal: Less than 100 mg/dL (<2.6 mmol/L).
  - Near optimal: 100 to 129 mg/dL.
  - Borderline high: 130 to 159 mg/dL.
  - High: 160 to 189 mg/dL.
  - Very high: 190 mg/dL or above.

- **High-Density Lipoprotein (HDL) Cholesterol ("Good" Cholesterol)**: Transports cholesterol away from arteries back to the liver for excretion.
  - Optimal (protective): 60 mg/dL or higher.
  - Acceptable for men: 40 to 59 mg/dL.
  - Acceptable for women: 50 to 59 mg/dL.
  - Low (risk factor): Less than 40 mg/dL in men; less than 50 mg/dL in women.

- **Triglycerides**: Most common type of fat stored in the body, derived from excess dietary calories.
  - Normal: Less than 150 mg/dL (<1.7 mmol/L).
  - Borderline high: 150 to 199 mg/dL.
  - High: 200 to 499 mg/dL.
  - Very high: 500 mg/dL or higher (carries risk of acute pancreatitis).

## 2. Lifestyle and Clinical Considerations
- Elevated triglycerides and LDL, combined with low HDL, increase atherosclerosis risk.
- Fasting for 9-12 hours is traditionally recommended prior to drawing a lipid panel, although non-fasting panels are increasingly utilized for standard screening.
